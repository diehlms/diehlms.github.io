Here I am, in Manila. My hotel residence is in the north part of town just before the big concentration starts to thin towards the jungle hills. It rains constantly, through sunshine and dark nights. It is a pleasant, warm rain that does not disturb nor slow the constant traffic outside my window. Life continues apace no matter the hour. In fact, when I wander out shortly before midnight to take the metro to some errand, the dank station tunnels are filled with a zoo of workers and revelers elbowing past each other to make the next upcoming train. The pipes hanging above, snaking their way through the station are covered in what is hopefully moss, but probably mold.

The train is obviously going the wrong way, for some reason I can't fathom, taking me further into the city center. I guess my errand was outside the city. It is unclear how I know what direction I originally intended.

I exit the station to street level, standing underneath opressive advertisements eclipsing the entire facades of apartment buildings. Backfiring mopeds and scooters buzz by. What looks like a rider mower is doing donuts in the intersection. On one side of the tractor is an American flag; presumably this is a John Deere, not like I know many other tractor brands (volvo?). A few kids are riding the mower, probably having a ton of fun.
