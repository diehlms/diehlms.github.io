Okay, yeah a week late and everyone has blasted their thoughts about the midterms out. Like a lot of people I know, I spent a good chunk of my waking hours on midterm Tuesday evening and Wednesday morning refreshing the increasingly filled in map of the US house and senate results. The hashed outlines of states yet to be filled in soon became bluer and bluer. The vast swath of American land was by Thursday an azure expanse. Governors, state houses, clerks, the whole gammut. There was a palpable sigh of relief that some abstract collection of things were going to change, but this time in a good direction.

The relief is real, but fleeting. I am glad that Greg Abbott is leaving office. I am glad that Vivek Ramaswamy wont hold office. I'm elated that Annie Andrews decesively won in South Carolina. I'm sort of in disbelief that Cyndie Hyde-Smith is on her way out in Mississipi. But the force of the electorate feels to have been saying something similar each election: life is getting worse, and the people in charge arent steering things effectively. We are still in an anti-incumbancy period, and it's a mistake to think next election voters will be more lenient towards those that do not act.

The diagnosis of why Republicans did so poorly is not hard to figure out. Trump and many Republicans won in 2024 because life was getting worse and more expensive. Hope was scarce. Winning in 2024 was rarely a reflection of policy choices you as a candidate were vouching for. The assumption the winners of 2024 made was that their ideas were what broke through to normal people. I am highly dubious of the assumption here. Likewise, I am dubious of the people who think they won in 2026 because of their ideas. Don't get me wrong, I am in favor of medicare-for-all, abortion access, and logical immigration reform. But the winner of 2026 tended to be the person against Data Centers, against masked agents in the street abducting our neighbors.

The next step after winning feels improbably hard to me, an armchair observer of politics. We are in a mess, and due for a reckoning. I fear it is time we start eating our vegetables, and take the wellfare of our countrymen gravely seriously. I dont think that requires taking down the economy at large, but things will probably need to slow before they accelerate again. Or maybe not.

The most unambiguously good thing to come out of the 2026 cycle was how limited cultural politics seemed to be in effective campaigns. My as of now largely unbaked idea is the use of cultural politics as an escape hatch from politicking on issues that affect people at a ycore level. Trans bathroom issues can rile people up in certain states, but riling people up on the same old crud can only work for so many campaigns before people realize that they too are engaging in a world poorer, less imaginative, and more stuck then the progress of their early years. And that is how Mississippi goes blue, and then back to red, and then blue again.

I expect the colors to strobe for a while, until we start making good progress.
