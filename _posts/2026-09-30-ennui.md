The word of the decade is ennui. The feeling of oppressive boredom which grips any motivation or actionable feeling in a death vice seems like a shared, lingering, global sensation. It is not AI's fault. It is not climate change's fault. It is not economic immobility's fault.

Perhaps it is an all of the above situation. But for now, I will focus on AI. Evidence suggests the big lab's frontier models are capable of solving or at least expanding our understanding of elusive mathematics problems, problems that have plagued and enticed budding mathematicians for years. Firing up a swarm of agents to iterate on a problem, given nearly infinite funding and compute, seems like it does "something" towards mathematic progress. I'm not a mathematician. For kicks, I took a look at one of the resulting papers of a proof and could not reason anything past the abstract.

Given I'm not a mathematician, the following is pure conjecture. I'd imagine the feeling of a mathematician toiling away for years, getting degrees based on solving a particular proof, delivering lectures and writing endless words on some niche graph theory subject becomes a white whale. The problem becomes the point of life, the goal of being able to push humanity's collective understanding of the world just a smidge past the high watermark becomes one of the reasons to get out of bed. Sure, there is some hubris involved to be able to attach one's name to history. But ego has led man to create amazing, beautiful objects to which we are all indebted. 

Why then, would I, a supposed mathematician, continue to strive if some nameless swarm of agents can put their nom de plume on the white whale problem, hundreds at a time. What then is the point? Maybe the frontier labs' goal is to rid humanity of our collective egos; good luck with that. With ego replaced with toil and rote, we can get back to just work. But if there is nothing to which we eventually can claim fame to, we get ennui. We get bored. We ask ourselves what's the point.
