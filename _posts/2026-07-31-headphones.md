I often walk around town with headphones, listening to music or podcasts. When I take the bus to work, I often read with headphones in tuned to classical music or ambient sounds to drown out the screech of the bus brakes and other passengers. Walking around with music has been a habit since at least college, where I would sneak in a few songs between classes, or walking to/from internships and jobs across D.C. The feeling of taking transit especially is cinematic, where you the passengar can star in a production of life being lived, where the unwitting audience bears tribute to the story in your head to which music serves a fairly epic backdrop. There wasn't much impetus for the headphones when it started, and listening to something has been an automated habit which I'm sure is not unique to me.
<br/><br/>
But popping in the headphones misses some of the point, I fear, of being awake and alive in a city.
