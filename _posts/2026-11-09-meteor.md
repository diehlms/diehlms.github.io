There was definitely something bad coming, like a meteor/asteroid. It was imminent and certain to strike near where some friends and I had holed up. The mood could only be described as ambivalent, since there was no screaming or frantic energy in the face of certain doom. I recall we were in a trench with a portrusion of concrete and soil jutting out right overhead. We took our position right before impact; it is not clear how we knew what time impact was expected but we did.

First it was quiet and still after the main collision. There was no flash of light or anything cinematicly epic about our view. We could only look out, past the direction the impacts debris were expected to travel. We had our back to the bomb. Seconds after impact, we saw the refuse and projectiles kicked up from the collision flying by like organic missiles. It was a sideways rain of shear forced earth jetison which we were powerless to do nothing but watch. It seemed like an unspoken truth that death could have come at any moment and squashed our crag halo, but alas everything settled after not too long. The heat of astral trash warmed our heads as we emerged.
