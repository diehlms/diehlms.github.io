I am an anxious person from time to time. My anxiety is not uniquely high, though it's difficult to say with certainty. I get overwhelmed from having either too much to do or too little to do at any given time. This I suppose is the cross to bear with being in a go-go world. As my tasks, responsibilities, and goals accumulate I notice my anxiety often gets to a point where it must be dealt with, like a blinking red button on a dashboard. Over time, my skills at dealing with the blinking red alarm have grown in tandem with my professional and social skills. Getting out of the house and having a run, as cliche as it is, helps tremendously. See too, nearly any form of exercise. I hate that it works, but it does.

Other forms of self soothing, like meditation and praying, work to various degrees. Everyone has their particular formula. One skill that has evaded me until recently I'm ashamed to admit has been to simply ask myself lots of questions, as a form of induced self-socratic learning.

I've been amazed how introspective, inquisitive, and calm it has made me simply by taking the time to ask questions like:

> why is THIS thing bothering me so much
> why is THIS thing so confusing and out of place
> why am I having THIS particular reaction
> what would be a reason for THIS thing to exist
> what would make a person do THAT thing

The important thing to keep in mind, as with meditation, is to reserve all judgements about both the questions and the answers. The goal with asking myself questions is to just monitor the way my mind wraps around my assumptions. Often times when I am in the most trouble it is because I have made assumptions about the answer to questions I did not wish to ask.

It bears fruit professionally as well, I might add. It's a mistake to assume the best work is done the fastest, as we remember from when professors and teachers preamble before handing out exams. With agentic programming, I often find myself in a doom loop of sorts: I prompt the LLM to do something, then get frustrated when the LLM can't discern exactly what I'm attempting, then go further down the rabbit hole of outlining and requirement describing. The doom loop is often a negative signal that I am making progress. Instead, when I notice significant friction between LLMs and what I expect and want to see, I stop and immediately start interrogating myself. The friction tells me something is constraining the world I want to see; some invisible understanding requires things to be so, and I am attempting to dissregard some foundational concept.

When I start asking myself questions, I am the only one who can answer. Searching for answers has become a theraputic exercise. Finding an answer is simply not that important. Finding the definitive answer is a fool's errand. Instead, asking myself why and how things are breaks me out of some assumption I thought was true.

It's easy to see if you do the crossword, especially on harder days. Staring at the board invites the brain to assume some things just are and cements answers in place. When I place the crossword down and come back, I often see the answer I put down as a guess, sometimes good and sometimes bad. Coming back is a form of asking questions, I suppose.
