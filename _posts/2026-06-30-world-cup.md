One of the movies I have rewatched the most is the official recap documentary of the 2006 World Cup held in Germany. I'm not even sure I'd recommend it, as unless you were a soccer-obsessed child like I was the movie now probably just plays as your typical sizzle reel. But I was a soccer-obsessed child right when the 2006 World Cup was going on, so I was captivated. I really did think Zinedine Zidane was evil to his core, not least because he has such a wickedly evil name, but also because of his famous head butt of some Italian player that I cannot remember, and will not look up. But I grew up, and now think Zidane is super cool, probably BECAUSE his name is so sinister. The 2006 World Cup was around the time I started to engage with players by name, and started to develop a somewhat parasocial relationship with my favorites, especially the US striker Landon Donovan. The guys I watched play were my heroes, and their arenas were inspiring.
<br/><br/>
One thing about soccer that seems to elude viewers who typically only watch sports like American Football, Basketball, Baseball, etc is that the box score of a soccer game really tells you very little about the reality of the match to a large extent. A 1-1 draw tells you almost nothing about the pacing, the momentum, and the tensions throughout the game. What astounds people is how exciting a 0-0 game can turn out to be, such as the Cape Verde & Spain match most recently. Obviously, it sucks when your country gets knocked out and has to go home, either in the knockout round or in group play. However, watching and experiencing the World Cup is and should be joyful. The pain of getting knocked out is fleeting. Getting knocked out of the World Cup is a singular, miniscule datapoint. Only a vanishingly small minority of countries have won the World Cup ever. It's not an embarresment to get knocked out.
<br/><br/>
The joy of the World Cup happens around the edges, like the excitement of the game itself. I love the fans coming from all corners of the earth to depleet a singular country of its beer, food, and attention. I love the idea of a country hosting all other countries, much like the Olympics. For the record, I love the Olympics for all the same reasons as the World Cup, but seeing as I have no personal connection to luge, archery, or the other sports the Olympics serves mostly as a novelty to me. At times my love for the World Cup is ineffible, I simply enjoy the vibe of it all. Other moments are more acute. I love when people represent the prototypical image of their country, like everyone on Croatia's name ending in `-ric`. Or Haaland looking legitametly like a viking, and he's from the viking country! Or the American striker being named "Landon Donovan". At times the World Cup makes me think our planet is simply a gigantic, casted orchestration and all events are scripted to be perfect for their time. This World Cup presented us with some uniquely American charm, like Lawrence, KS adopting the team from Algeria. When an Algerian news crew interviewed a local Lawrentian about what he thought of Algeria, he gave a cheerful rendition of the top wikipedia bullet points of Algerian history. It almost made me weep. Lawrence, KS should be the capital of the United States.
<br/><br/>
The World Cup is not without it's cynics, both within and outside FIFA. I don't care. Or rather, I don't dwell on it. FIFA could burn to a smoldering heap of ashes. So long as our world continues to have a hosted series of soccer games where all who are good enough can play, there is something good and great.
